If you want to sound professional in HR and office settings, it's useful to replace casual language with formal business phrases. Here are common scenarios with examples.

## 1. Greeting a colleague or HR

**Casual:**

* Hi
* Hey

**Formal:**

* Good morning.
* Good afternoon.
* I hope you're doing well.
* I hope this email finds you well.

---

## 2. Requesting leave

**Casual:**

* I need leave tomorrow.

**Formal:**

* I would like to request leave for tomorrow.
* I am writing to request one day's leave due to a personal commitment.
* Kindly approve my leave request.

---

## 3. Asking for help

**Casual:**

* Can you help me?

**Formal:**

* Could you please assist me with this?
* I would appreciate your guidance.
* Would you be able to help me with this matter?

---

## 4. Following up

**Casual:**

* Any update?

**Formal:**

* I would like to follow up regarding my previous request.
* Could you please provide an update at your earliest convenience?
* Kindly let me know the current status.

---

## 5. Reporting completion

**Casual:**

* I finished it.

**Formal:**

* The task has been completed.
* I have completed the assigned work.
* The requested action has been carried out.

---

## 6. Apologizing

**Casual:**

* Sorry I'm late.

**Formal:**

* I sincerely apologize for the delay.
* Please accept my apologies for the inconvenience.
* Thank you for your patience and understanding.

---

## 7. Asking for clarification

**Casual:**

* I don't understand.

**Formal:**

* Could you please clarify this point?
* I would appreciate further clarification.
* Would you mind explaining this in more detail?

---

## 8. Meeting phrases

**Starting a meeting**

* Thank you all for joining.
* Let's begin today's meeting.
* The purpose of today's meeting is...

**During the meeting**

* I'd like to add a point.
* I agree with your suggestion.
* I have a different perspective.
* Could you elaborate on that?
* That's a valid point.

**Ending the meeting**

* Thank you for your valuable time.
* We will proceed with the agreed action items.
* Please let me know if you have any further questions.

---

## 9. HR Interview phrases

**Introducing yourself**

* Thank you for the opportunity to interview.
* I appreciate your time today.

**Talking about experience**

* I have experience in...
* I was responsible for...
* I successfully managed...
* I collaborated with cross-functional teams.

**Talking about strengths**

* I am a quick learner.
* I have strong communication skills.
* I work well under pressure.
* I am detail-oriented.

---

## 10. Email phrases

**Beginning**

* Dear Sir/Madam,
* Dear Mr./Ms. Smith,
* I hope you are doing well.
* I am writing regarding...

**Ending**

* Thank you for your time and consideration.
* I look forward to your response.
* Please feel free to contact me if you need any further information.
* Kind regards,
* Best regards,
* Sincerely,

---

## 12. Powerful business vocabulary

* Appreciate
* Coordinate
* Facilitate
* Prioritize
* Implement
* Resolve
* Collaborate
* Escalate
* Confirm
* Schedule
* Discuss
* Review
* Proceed
* Notify
* Acknowledge
* Maintain
* Support
* Evaluate
* Recommend
* Deliver

### Example

> **Manager:** Could you provide an update on the project?

> **Employee:** Certainly. The project is progressing as planned. We have completed the initial phase and expect to finish the remaining tasks by Friday. Please let me know if you require any additional information.

Learning a few of these phrases and using them consistently can make your communication sound much more polished in HR interactions, interviews, meetings, emails, and everyday office conversations. If you're preparing for a corporate job, practicing these expressions aloud will also help them come naturally.
